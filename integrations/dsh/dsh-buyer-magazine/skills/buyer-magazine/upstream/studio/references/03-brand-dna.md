# 03 · Brand DNA

## Contract

**Input:** supplier/brand site, buyer profile, publication purpose, and permitted assets. **Output:** a short editorial identity sidecar, not a copied website skin. **Consumer:** copy, art direction, typography, and layout. The magazine has its own reading hierarchy while accurately identifying source businesses.

## Separate the publication's promise from the source's identity

Write four decisions before styling:

1. **Reader:** role, category, destination market, and buying horizon.
2. **Promise:** the specific decision this issue helps make.
3. **Voice:** three concrete constraints, such as “plain English, technical when useful, cautious about unverified claims”.
4. **Visual behavior:** the balance of documentary product imagery, quiet information tables, and editorial framing.

For this package, default to **Supply Notes**: a calm, informed procurement publication, designed for a short coffee break. Use warm paper, ink, restrained coral accents, serif editorial headlines, and sans-serif specifications. Avoid turning the source company's every color and promotional slogan into the magazine interface.

## Extract source identity with evidence

Record legal name if available, public brand name, product focus, market positioning, logo source and permission, and official contact links. Keep “brand”, “manufacturer”, “trading company”, and “retailer” distinct. A factory-looking website or a Chinese address does not establish factory ownership. Say “supplier” or the sourced role when uncertain.

Extract repeatable visual characteristics from actual source material: product form, material color, image background, lighting, packaging legibility, and shot types. Use these to choose crops and pairing. Do not infer product quality from photography quality.

## Adapt the editorial emphasis

| Source type | Lead with | Preserve caution about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manufacturer | Production scope, exact product capability, sample pathway | Capacity, certifications, factory ownership |
| Consumer brand | Product differentiation, assortment, retail benchmark | Wholesale authorization and trade pricing |
| Material supplier | Grade, dimensions, process compatibility, test evidence | Substituting similar grades |
| OEM/ODM provider | Documented customization scope and quotation inputs | Bespoke promises without a specification |
| Distributor | Available assortment, region, published fulfillment terms | Presenting distributor claims as factory facts |

## Specify identity as decisions, not adjectives

Weak: “premium, modern, elegant”. Useful: “one serif display family; no gradients; off-white page; 2:1 lead image with visible product silhouette; specifications on rules rather than stacked dashboard cards; one primary sourcing action per story”.

Set a headline style, evidence-label vocabulary, image treatment, and sponsorship treatment. Give reader-facing controls consistent verbs: “Save”, “Compare”, “View sources”, “Prepare inquiry”. Do not switch between four expressions for the same action.

## Gate

Require a reader, a decision promise, a sourced identity statement, and a concrete type/image/layout recipe. Confirm that the issue would still be legible with the supplier logo removed: visual authority must come from editorial structure and evidence, not borrowed branding.

**Example:** for a decorative homeware site, lead with material, finish, dimensions, assortment role and missing trade terms. For an electrical appliance supplier, lead with rated power, voltage/market compatibility, documented tests and sample validation. The same porcelain design can support both, but the procurement information hierarchy changes.
